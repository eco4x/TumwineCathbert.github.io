02 / The journey so far

Still learning. Still showing up.

A personal record of the curiosity, constraints, lessons and small steps that continue to shape my path into technology and cybersecurity.

This is not a story about having perfect equipment or a straight path. It is about starting with what I had, learning as I went, finding ways to keep practising, and gradually building the skills and confidence to do more. I will keep adding to this journal as the journey unfolds — including the notes, resources and practical lessons that may help someone following a similar path.

2023 · THE BEGINNING

Linux in my pocket.

My early exploration of Linux began on an Android phone using Termux. I did not have a laptop, so I started with the device I had — learning to navigate the terminal, understand commands and become more comfortable with a Linux environment.

It was an early step, not an expert setup. But it gave me a place to start and made technology feel something I could explore for myself.

What stayed with me: You do not need ideal resources to begin learning. Start with what is available and keep building.

THE CONTINUING JOURNEY

Learning with the equipment I have.

Over time, I gained access to better equipment, but the journey has never depended on having the newest machine. At this stage, I am still learning and showing up with a second-hand computer that is around 16 years old and has a dead battery.

It has limitations, and I have to work around them. I share this because progress is not always made in perfect conditions. Sometimes it means being patient, adapting your workflow and making the most of the tools in front of you while working toward something better.

What I am learning: Constraints can shape how you approach a problem. They do not have to decide whether you keep going.

Illustrative image — not a photograph of my actual computer.
A CHANGE IN MINDSET

From collecting certificates to applying knowledge.

As I continued learning, I began to understand that certificates can recognise effort and structured study, but they are not the same as practical ability. What matters is whether I can explain what I learned, apply it, test it, recognise mistakes and improve.

That is why I am putting more emphasis on hands-on labs, documented configurations, project work and honest reflections — not just the names of courses I have completed.

My principle: Learn it. Practise it. Understand it. Document what worked, what failed and what you would do differently.
